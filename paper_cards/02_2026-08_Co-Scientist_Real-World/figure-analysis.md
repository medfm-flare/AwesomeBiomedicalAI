# Figure-by-figure analysis: Co-Scientist in the Real World

All images are faithful full-page renders from arXiv:2608.26701v1. They retain the original captions, panels, axes and neighbouring context. The interpretations below do not convert a figure into independent validation.

## Figure 1 — workflow and autonomy levels

![Figure 1](figures/page-003.png)

The blue, green and yellow bands separate ideation, experimentation and writing; the domain columns align inputs, execution environments and outputs with an autonomy axis. It is a useful template for showing where humans intervene. It does not show that all three domains are fully autonomous. [Paper: PDF p.3, Figure 1]

## Figure 2 — candidate-to-physical-evidence chain

![Figure 2](figures/page-012.png)

The panels progress from human-agent iteration to the experimental platform and then XRD, microscopy, elemental and lattice evidence. This supports an experimentally grounded candidate workflow, but the paper itself states that atomic structure remains unconfirmed. [Paper: PDF pp.12, 15]

## Figure 3 — speed and product quality together

![Figure 3](figures/page-014.png)

Hardware workflow, microscopy and Raman evidence are paired with a material-by-inference-mode matrix. Rapid inference succeeds but produces smaller or less regular domains. The design lesson is to plot time beside outcome quality, not as a substitute for it. [Paper: PDF p.14, Figure 3]

## Figure 4 — observed input versus generated phenotype

![Figure 4](figures/page-017.png)

The page distinguishes measured colony images from 16 generated candidates and model-based selection. Visual plausibility is an intermediate result, not proof that the biological mechanism was identified. [Paper: PDF pp.17, 81]

## Figure 5 — qualitative similarity followed by quantitative error

![Figure 5](figures/page-019.png)

Measured/generated examples are followed by four aligned morphology plots. The circularity panel exposes a discrepancy hidden by qualitative resemblance. Non-significant interaction tests are not equivalence tests. [Paper: PDF pp.18-19]

## Figure 6 — search, elimination and revision topology

![Figure 6](figures/page-022.png)

Parallel answer blocks, a pairwise elimination tree and iterative review loops make the 40-80-call inference structure visible. Boxes show orchestration, not isolated causal evidence for every component or hard security permissions. [Paper: PDF pp.21-22]

## Table 2 — judge-specific and length-corrected scoring

![Table 2](figures/page-023.png)

Raw and corrected scores are shown for two model judges. It demonstrates rubric-dependent performance and the importance of length correction, but lacks an equal-compute repeated-sampling baseline. [Paper: PDF p.23]

## Figure 7 — physician preference and agreement

![Figure 7](figures/page-025.png)

Preference/tie distributions are displayed beside human-model agreement by dimension. Most physician-rated dimensions do not significantly improve; the plotted kappa reference is a figure convention, not a universal clinical threshold. [Paper: PDF pp.24-25]

## Figure 8 — error categories and remaining failures

![Figure 8](figures/page-029.png)

The repeated three-condition panels separate result, method, plagiarism/attribution and safety outcomes. The key design lesson is to preserve denominators and several failure classes. A zero in 50 papers would not prove zero population risk. [Paper: PDF pp.28-29]
