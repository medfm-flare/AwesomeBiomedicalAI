## Terminology Ledger

| Canonical term | First-use definition | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| HEX | "H&E to protein expression" — the model that predicts 40 protein biomarker values per H&E image tile | The **image-translation** model. All protein-prediction results belong to it |
| MICA | "multimodal integration via co-attention" — a second, separately trained model that fuses real H&E features with HEX-generated virtual CODEX features | The **outcome-prediction** model. All prognosis and immunotherapy results belong to it, not to HEX alone |
| virtual spatial proteomics / virtual CODEX | HEX's output: a synthetic multi-channel protein-expression map reconstructed by tiling per-tile predictions across a slide | Never a measurement; always a prediction |
| MUSK | the pathology vision–language foundation model used as HEX's frozen-then-partially-tuned backbone, and as MICA's H&E feature extractor | Backbone, not contribution; CONCH was also tested and is worse |
| CODEX / PhenoCycler | co-detection by indexing, run on the PhenoCycler 2.0 platform — cyclic immunofluorescence giving >50 proteins at single-cell resolution on one section | The **ground truth** for HEX. Vendor names are used interchangeably in the paper |
| WSI | whole-slide image | Ten of these constitute the entire HEX training set |
| tile | a 224 × 224-pixel H&E patch at 40× (~50–56 µm) whose *mean* CODEX intensity per marker is the regression target | The unit of prediction. HEX predicts a tile average, **not** single cells |
| TMA core | a small tissue-microarray core; three of the six clinical cohorts are core-based rather than whole-slide | Cores and patients are different denominators and the paper uses both |
| FDS | feature distribution smoothing — Gaussian smoothing of penultimate-layer feature statistics across target bins, to counter marker imbalance | Ablated; removing it degrades all metrics |
| ALF | adaptive loss function — a robust loss with learnable shape α and scale c | Ablated; removing it degrades all metrics |
| DINOv2 | a natural-image self-supervised model (142M natural images) used to featurize each *virtual CODEX channel* inside MICA | Notable: a natural-image encoder reads a synthetic protein map |
| integrated gradients | the attribution method used to rank tiles by contribution to MICA's risk score | The basis of every biological-interpretation claim |
| SCS | spatial co-localization signature — normalized Jaccard index of two marker-defined cell states over tiles | Six pre-specified pairs, nominal *P*, no multiplicity correction |
| Stanford-WSI / Stanford-TMA / TA-TMA / Bern | the four CODEX–H&E datasets: 10 WSIs (training), 264 cores, 108 cores, 206 pan-cancer cores | Only Stanford-WSI trains HEX |
| NLST / TCGA / PLCO / Stanford-IO | the outcome cohorts: NLST trains MICA for prognosis; Stanford-IO (n = 148) is the only immunotherapy cohort | Stanford-IO is internally cross-validated, not externally validated |

## 02 One-Sentence Summary

[Paper] HEX regresses 40 CODEX protein-expression values from each H&E tile using a MUSK pathology-foundation-model backbone, reaching mean Pearson *r* 0.790 in cross-validation on ten whole slides and 0.738 on 372 independent TMA cores; a **separate** model, MICA, then fuses real H&E with HEX's synthetic protein maps by co-attention and is what actually delivers the paper's clinical headlines — a 22% relative gain in prognostic C-index over clinical variables and a 24–39% relative gain in immunotherapy-response AUC over PD-L1 and TMB. [Paper: Abstract; Results, "Cross-validation performance"; Results, "HEX improves prognosis prediction in early-stage lung cancer"]

## 03 Research Question

- [Paper] Spatial proteomics is transformative but too costly, complex and low-throughput for clinical translation; H&E is universal. Can spatially resolved multi-protein expression be *generated* from standard histopathology accurately enough to be clinically useful? [Paper: Main]
- [Paper] A secondary question the authors raise explicitly: previous molecular-from-H&E work trained at the **bulk tissue level**, which forces weakly supervised learning from few samples and cannot resolve intratumor heterogeneity. Same-section spatial proteomics supplies a dense, tile-level supervision signal instead. [Paper: Main]
- [Analysis] The framing question the paper does not pose but its own results answer is: how much of the clinical gain comes from the *predicted proteins*, and how much from having a second, differently-encoded view of the same H&E? The unimodal ablations bear directly on this and are discussed in 11 and 13.

## 04 Research Background and Development Path

1. [Paper] CODEX and related high-plex platforms can quantify >50 proteins at single-cell resolution across a whole section, but require specialized instruments and trained personnel, and existing spatial-proteomics studies are consequently limited to small sample numbers. [Paper: Main; Discussion]
2. [Paper] Deep learning can already estimate mutations and gene expression from H&E, but at bulk-tissue supervision, which is both a statistical and a biological limitation. [Paper: Main]
3. [Paper] Prior H&E-to-protein work used unpaired or paired H&E/IHC, paired IHC and multiplexed immunofluorescence, or autofluorescence — and was restricted to a handful of markers, with no large-cohort outcome validation. [Paper: Main]
4. [Paper] Spatial-transcriptomics-from-H&E prediction has been attempted and performs at average Pearson *r* ≈ 0.2, which the authors attribute to noisy, high-dimensional transcript data. [Paper: Discussion]
5. [Analysis] The enabling move is experimental, not architectural. Staining **the same 5 µm section** first with PhenoCycler and then with H&E, and registering the two with PALOM, converts a hard cross-section correspondence problem into a within-section one — and that is what makes 755,000 densely supervised tile pairs available from only ten slides. The model on top is a three-layer regression head.

## 06 Core Idea

- **Surface method:** a frozen-then-partially-fine-tuned pathology foundation model plus a small multi-output regression head, trained on same-section CODEX/H&E tile pairs.
- **Core insight:** the supervision, not the network. Same-section CODEX-then-H&E staining plus subcellular-accuracy registration turns one slide into ~75,000 labelled examples, so a **ten-slide** experiment yields a training set large enough to fit a 40-output regressor. The two training tricks — FDS for target imbalance, ALF for outlier robustness — exist because the labels are assay measurements rather than curated annotations, and both are shown to be necessary. [Paper: Methods, "HEX model architecture and training strategy"; Results, "Independent validation performance"]
- **Second, separable idea:** MICA. Rather than concatenating modalities late, virtual-CODEX features are projected to queries and real-H&E features to keys and values, so the *predicted proteome guides attention over the histology*. This is the model that produces every clinical number. [Paper: Methods, "Model architecture for integrating histology and virtual spatial proteomics"]
- **General lesson:** [Analysis] when a generated modality is a deterministic function of the input modality, "multimodal fusion" is really a structured re-encoding of one input. That does not make it useless — the results show it is not — but it changes what the gain can be attributed to, and it means the two "modalities" cannot fail independently.

## 07 Method Overview

**Data generation.** 5 µm FFPE sections → PhenoCycler 2.0, 40-antibody panel, 20× objective, DAPI/AF488/Atto550/AF647 → flow cell removed → **the same section** H&E-stained and scanned on a Leica Aperio AT2 at 40×. CODEX DAPI is registered to the H&E hematoxylin channel with PALOM (full-resolution affine initialization from 4,000 keypoints, block-wise shift refinement). Registration accuracy: **99.2% of nearest-neighbour cell–cell distances fall below the median nucleus diameter**. Registered images are cut into non-overlapping 224 × 224 tiles and each marker's mean intensity in the tile becomes the target. [Paper: Methods, "CODEX with high-plex PhenoCycler imaging"; Methods, "CODEX and H&E image registration and dataset construction"]

**HEX.** MUSK backbone → linear(→256) + ReLU + dropout → linear(→128) + ReLU + dropout → linear(→40). Trained with Adam, lr 1 × 10⁻⁵, exponential decay 0.95, batch 384, dropout 0.5, 120 epochs: the last four encoder layers plus heads for 100 epochs, then heads only with the backbone fully frozen for 20. Augmentation: flips, rotations, brightness/contrast/saturation/hue jitter. Inference slides a 224-pixel window and stitches the tiles into a virtual CODEX map. A **separate high-resolution variant** was retrained on a 1/20 subset with a 14-pixel prediction window for visualization. [Paper: Methods, "HEX model training details"]

**MICA.** CLAM tissue segmentation → 384 × 384 tiles at 20× → MUSK features (H&E bag) and DINOv2 `dinov2_vits14_reg` features per virtual-CODEX channel (protein bag) → CODEX-guided co-attention (Q from proteins, K and V from histology) → two modality-specific multiple-instance-learning transformers with global average pooling → discrete-time survival loss (prognosis) or cross-entropy (objective response). Both feature extractors frozen; batch size 1 with eight gradient-accumulation steps; 20 epochs; no early stopping. [Paper: Methods, "Model architecture for integrating histology and virtual spatial proteomics"; Methods, "Training and evaluation of MICA"]

**Main workflow figure:** Fig. 1a,b and Extended Data Fig. 1. No figure image is reproduced here; see `figure-analysis.md`.

## 08 Core Module Breakdown

| Module | Function | Why needed | Input → output | Evidence | Removal boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same-section CODEX→H&E protocol | Creates paired labels without cross-section mismatch | Adjacent sections do not contain the same cells | Section → two co-registered images | [Paper: Methods, "H&E staining of the same section after CODEX"] | Not ablated; it is the study's premise |
| PALOM registration | Aligns CODEX to H&E at subcellular scale | Tile labels are only meaningful if aligned | Two WSIs → transform | [Paper: Methods; Supplementary Fig. 30] | Validated (99.2% < median nucleus diameter), not ablated |
| MUSK backbone | Visual features for H&E tiles | Ten slides cannot train an encoder from scratch | Tile → embedding | [Paper: Methods, "HEX model training details"] | **Ablated**: CONCH backbone is faster but less accurate |
| Three-layer regression head | 40 simultaneous outputs | One forward pass per tile for the whole panel | Embedding → 40 values | [Paper: Extended Data Fig. 1] | Not ablated |
| FDS | Calibrates features across target bins | Marker abundance is heavily imbalanced | Penultimate features → smoothed features | [Paper: Methods; Supplementary Figs. 3, 4] | **Ablated**: removal degrades all metrics |
| ALF | Robust loss with learnable α, c | CODEX carries imaging noise and outliers | Residual → loss | [Paper: Methods; Supplementary Figs. 3, 4] | **Ablated**: removal degrades all metrics |
| Sliding-window stitching | Reconstructs a whole-slide protein map | Prediction is per tile | Tiles → virtual CODEX WSI | [Paper: Methods, "HEX model training details"] | 224-px stride default; 14-px variant retrained separately |
| DINOv2 channel encoder (MICA) | Featurizes each virtual protein channel | Protein maps are images, not vectors | Channel → embedding | [Paper: Methods, "Training and evaluation of MICA"] | Not ablated against a pathology encoder |
| CODEX-guided co-attention (MICA) | Protein map steers attention over histology | Early fusion beats late fusion | (Q, K, V) → fused representation | [Paper: Methods; Supplementary Fig. 14] | **Benchmarked** against PORPOISE late fusion |
| Integrated gradients | Ranks tiles by risk contribution | Enables the biological interpretation | Tile → attribution | [Paper: Methods, "Biological interpretation of outcome predictions"] | Post hoc; changes no prediction |

## 09 Essential Formulas and Symbols

Two families of equation are stated. **FDS** calibrates penultimate features *h* within target bin *b* as `h̄ = C̄_b^{1/2} C_b^{-1/2}(h − μ_b) + μ̄_b`, where μ_b and C_b are the bin mean and covariance and the smoothed statistics μ̄_b, C̄_b are Gaussian-kernel weighted sums `μ̄_b = Σ_{m∈B} g(y_b, y_m) μ_m` over bins, updated by exponential moving average. **ALF** is `L(r, α, c) = ρ(r, α, c) + log Z(α)` with `ρ(r, α, c) = (|α−2|/α)·(((r/c)²/(α−2) + 1)^{α/2} − 1)`, where *r* is the residual, α the learnable shape, *c* the learnable scale and Z(α) the partition function; both α and c are initialized to 1. **MICA's** co-attention is standard scaled dot-product attention `H̃_coa = softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V` with the asymmetry that `Q = M_bag P_q` comes from the protein bag while `K, V` come from the histology bag. Reported metrics: Pearson *r*, Spearman *r*, SSIM and MSE for protein prediction; C-index, hazard ratio and log-rank for survival; AUC for objective response; normalized Jaccard index for spatial co-localization. [Paper: Methods, "HEX model architecture and training strategy"; Methods, "Model architecture for integrating histology and virtual spatial proteomics"; Methods, "Statistical analysis"]

## 11 Correct Interpretation of the Conclusions

- **Two models, two claim families.** HEX predicts proteins; MICA predicts outcomes. Figs. 4, 5 and 6 are all titled for **MICA**. The abstract's "HEX-enabled multimodal integration improved prognostic accuracy by 22% and immunotherapy response prediction by 24–39%" is a MICA result that consumes HEX as an input. No prognosis or response number on this page belongs to HEX alone. [Paper: Fig. 4; Fig. 5; Fig. 6]
- **The virtual-CODEX-only model is the number that tells you what the proteins contribute.** For prognosis it reaches C-index 0.59 against H&E-only 0.56 and MICA 0.68; for response, AUC 0.75 against 0.72 and 0.82. The generated proteome alone is a weak predictor; most of the gain is in the fusion. [Paper: Fig. 4b; Fig. 6a]
- **"22%" is relative, and it is 0.71 versus 0.58** for MICA-plus-clinical against clinical variables alone, pooled across cohorts and weighted by patient count. "24–39%" is AUC 0.82 against PD-L1 0.66 and TMB 0.59. TMB's 95% CI is **0.37–0.80** — it does not exclude chance, so the 39% figure is a comparison against an uninformative comparator in this cohort. [Paper: Results; Fig. 6a]
- **Prognosis is externally validated; immunotherapy is not.** MICA for prognosis is trained on NLST and tested on four untouched cohorts with cutoffs fixed in advance — a strong design. The immunotherapy result is fivefold cross-validation inside the single 148-patient Stanford-IO cohort. The abstract's "six independent cohorts totaling 2,298 patients" merges these two very different evidence regimes. [Paper: Methods, "Training and evaluation of MICA"]
- **The 12-cancer pan-cancer prognosis result is also cross-validation**, not independent validation. [Paper: Methods, "Training and evaluation of MICA"]
- **HEX predicts a tile mean, not single cells.** CODEX resolves single cells; HEX's target is the average marker intensity over a ~50 µm tile. The 14-pixel high-resolution outputs come from a *different* model retrained on a 1/20 data subset and are presented for visualization. "Virtual spatial proteomics" should not be read as virtual single-cell proteomics. [Paper: Methods, "CODEX and H&E image registration and dataset construction"; Methods, "HEX model training details"]
- **Accuracy degrades predictably with distance from the training distribution:** 0.790 (CV, same ten slides) → 0.738 (Stanford TMA cores) → 0.658 (Bern, different scanner and protocol, 24 markers) → 0.566 (CRC retrained from scratch) → 0.479–0.606 (IHC, n = 3).
- **The virtual-versus-true CODEX equivalence is a null result.** "No statistically significant difference" in two modest cohorts is absence of evidence; no equivalence margin was pre-specified. [Paper: Supplementary Fig. 16]
- **The biological interpretation is confirmatory, not exploratory.** Six marker pairs were chosen from prior literature; nominal *P* values are reported without multiplicity correction, which the authors state and justify by the small pre-specified hypothesis set. The strongest evidence that the biology is real is Extended Data Fig. 10, where the same prognostic patterns reproduce using **measured** CODEX. [Paper: Methods, "Biological interpretation of outcome predictions"; Extended Data Fig. 10]
- **The Discussion's treatment-selection percentages** — 40% of PD-L1-high patients without durable response, 61% of PD-L1-negative without benefit, 29% of EGFR-mutant patients with durable benefit — are descriptive statistics from the 148-patient cross-validated cohort, offered as hypotheses for combination strategies, not as validated decision rules. [Paper: Discussion]

## 12 Limitations Explicitly Acknowledged by the Authors

- [Paper] "One limitation of current spatial proteomics platforms is the reliance on antibodies for immunofluorescence imaging, which may limit the number of potential targets." [Paper: Discussion]
- [Paper] "The protein biomarker panel may need to be optimized for different cancers or diseases." [Paper: Discussion]
- [Paper] Spatial transcriptomics, by contrast, profiles the whole transcriptome and gives a more comprehensive picture of tissue architecture; the protein panel is targeted and therefore narrower. [Paper: Discussion]
- [Paper] Prior H&E-to-spatial-transcriptomics work reaches only *r* ≈ 0.2 "partly due to noisy and high-dimensional gene expression data" — the authors present their own 0.73–0.79 as measured on a targeted protein panel, not a comparable target. [Paper: Discussion]
- [Paper] Multiplicity: only six pre-specified biologically informed marker pairs were tested, and "nominal *P* values were reported without correction for multiple hypothesis testing." [Paper: Methods, "Biological interpretation of outcome predictions"]
- [Paper] Kaplan–Meier analyses of co-expression patterns used log-rank tests "with no multiple-comparison adjustment", and multivariable Cox *P* values are stated to be unadjusted. [Paper: Fig. 4d; Fig. 5e]
- [Paper] Scaling analyses show diminishing returns beyond roughly eight to ten WSIs and roughly 20 markers — an acknowledged ceiling on what more data of the same kind would buy. [Paper: Results, "Spatial transcriptomics comparison and scaling analyses"]
- [Paper] The study is retrospective and IRB-approved at a single coordinating institution. [Paper: Methods, "Study design"]
- [Analysis] The Discussion contains no limitation paragraph proper. It does not name the ten-patient training set, the single-centre immunotherapy cohort, the cross-validated pan-cancer result, or the absence of prospective evaluation. Those appear in 13 as this card's own critique, not as author-stated limits.

## 13 Critical Analysis

| [Analysis] Observation | Potential issue | Why it matters | Test | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The entire HEX training set is **ten patients from one hospital** | 755,000 tiles is a large *n* of correlated observations, not a large *n* of biological samples; five-fold CV over ten patients gives eight-patient training folds | Panel-wide accuracy could be dominated by ten individuals' staining, fixation and morphology | Leave-one-patient-out with per-patient variance reported; add slides from a second scanner and institution to training | [Paper: Methods, "CODEX and H&E image registration and dataset construction"] |

## 14 Knowledge Learned

- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: when a paper contains two models, attribute every headline to the model that produced it. Here the protein-prediction claims are HEX's and the clinical claims are MICA's, and the figure titles say so even where the abstract does not.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: a generated modality is not an independent modality. Fusing H&E with something predicted from H&E cannot add information, only structure — so the right control is a same-architecture, same-parameter-count model with two encoders over the *same* input.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: report the unimodal ablation next to the fusion result. The virtual-CODEX-only C-index of 0.59 is the single most informative number in the prognosis section and it is easy to skim past.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: same-section, sequential multi-assay staining is a powerful way to manufacture dense supervision — one slide became ~75,000 labelled tiles here, and it turned a ten-patient experiment into a trainable dataset.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: validate a generative molecular predictor against an assay from a *different* physical family. CODEX-to-CODEX agreement shares artefacts; the IHC check is the one that does not, and it is the weakest and smallest experiment in the paper.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: quantify the accuracy of the exact model that produced the figures. A visualization variant retrained on 1/20 of the data needs its own metrics.
- Agent-derived knowledge candidate: an accuracy cascade across increasing domain shift (0.790 → 0.738 → 0.658 → 0.566 → 0.479) is more useful to a prospective user than any single headline correlation, and this paper supplies all five points.

## 15 Connections to Existing Knowledge

[Analysis] HEX sits at the junction of three lines. From **pathology foundation models** it takes MUSK, with CONCH as the tested alternative, and inherits the now-standard tile-embedding-plus-MIL framing; MICA's multiple-instance-learning transformers and its PORPOISE late-fusion comparator come from the same computational-pathology literature. From **image-to-image translation** it takes its baselines — DeepLIIF (conditional GAN) and Virtual Multiplexer (contrastive unpaired translation) — and its central departure: HEX is a *regressor over assay values*, not a generator of images, which is why it can be trained with a robust loss and calibrated features rather than an adversarial objective. From **spatial omics prediction** it inherits the H&E-to-spatial-transcriptomics problem (BLEEP, OmiCLIP), and its argument against that line is empirical — a targeted 40-protein panel is learnable at *r* ≈ 0.73–0.79 while the whole transcriptome is not, though the two targets are not commensurable. Within this catalogue it pairs with the other clinical-translation entries: like **P-CARE** it carries a model toward a treatment decision, but where P-CARE completed a CLIA-grade assay and a registered randomized trial, HEX stops at retrospective validation; unlike **AlphaGenome** and **Evo 2**, whose claim boundaries are drawn by which model produced which number, HEX's boundary is drawn by which of *its two* models produced which number — and the abstract blurs it.

**Open catalogue question (a) — routing.** CONTRIBUTING rule 1 sends a model built for one imaging modality to that modality's page, and by that rule HEX belongs on `pathology.md`: its input is H&E, its backbone is a pathology foundation model, its baselines are histology stain-translation methods, and MICA's whole apparatus is computational-pathology machinery. It currently sits under Proteomics & Protein Design because the *prediction target* is a proteomic readout, validated against CODEX/PhenoCycler and interpreted through protein co-expression niches. The full text supports both readings and settles neither: the proteomic content is genuine and load-bearing (the Bern 57-plex external validation and the CRC 33-novel-marker extension are proteomics experiments), while the deployment surface is entirely histopathological. A defensible resolution is a primary listing on `pathology.md` with a cross-reference here. **This needs agreeing with Leo Yin before any move.**

**Open catalogue question (b) — authorship.** Middle author **Yuchen Li is designated co-first with Zhe Li**, but the catalogue schema records first and last authors only, so the co-first designation is currently invisible in `AI4biology.yaml`. This card records it explicitly in section 01. Either the schema gains an optional `co_first` field, or the `note:` field carries it; either way the fact should not be lost to the schema's shape.

## 16 Research Ideas

### Agent-derived research candidate 1

**Is the fusion gain informational or architectural?** [Hypothesis] MICA's improvement over the H&E-only baseline (C-index 0.68 versus 0.56; response AUC 0.82 versus 0.72) is driven substantially by *architecture and effective ensembling* rather than by protein-specific content, because the virtual CODEX map is a deterministic function of the same H&E input and can carry no information the histology does not already contain. Delta: build a control model identical to MICA in every respect — co-attention, two MIL transformer branches, parameter count, optimizer, schedule — but replace the virtual-CODEX branch with (i) MUSK features of the same tiles under a different augmentation, (ii) a randomly initialized frozen encoder over the H&E tiles, and (iii) 40 channels of *shuffled* virtual CODEX in which marker identity is permuted per slide. Validation: run all four variants under the published protocol — NLST training with fixed cutoffs, external testing on TCGA, PLCO, Stanford-TMA and TA-TMA, plus fivefold cross-validation on Stanford-IO — and report the C-index and AUC gap over H&E-only for each, with bootstrap confidence intervals on the *differences*, not on the point estimates. Falsifier: if the shuffled-marker and different-augmentation controls recover most of MICA's margin over H&E-only, the protein semantics are not what is doing the work and the "virtual spatial proteomics improves outcome prediction" framing does not survive. Failure modes: shuffling within a slide may preserve the joint distribution well enough to keep genuine protein signal; the control encoders may be systematically weaker for reasons unrelated to content; the small Stanford-IO cohort will not resolve modest differences. Innovation status: unverified; prior-art search required.

### Agent-derived research candidate 2

**A prospective, assay-anchored test of virtual against measured spatial proteomics.** [Hypothesis] Virtual CODEX is non-inferior to measured CODEX for MICA-based outcome prediction — the paper's strongest claim, currently supported only by a non-significant difference in two small retrospective cohorts with no equivalence margin — and it will hold at a pre-specified margin of 0.03 C-index in a cohort large enough to test it, while HEX's *per-marker* fidelity will prove to be the binding constraint, with the low-correlation markers concentrating the failures. Delta: collect a prospective NSCLC cohort with same-section CODEX and H&E and mature outcome data, pre-register a non-inferiority margin and power the study for it, then stratify the analysis by each marker's independently measured Pearson *r* to test whether prediction error at the marker level propagates to outcome error at the patient level. Validation: co-primary endpoints are (1) the non-inferiority test of MICA-Virtual against MICA-True on C-index and (2) the slope of outcome-error against marker-level prediction error; secondary endpoints replicate the six pre-specified spatial co-localization signatures using measured CODEX, extending Extended Data Fig. 10 beyond the three pairs tested there; add an orthogonal IHC arm on tens of cases rather than three, since IHC is the only check that does not share CODEX's assay artefacts. Falsifier: MICA-Virtual fails non-inferiority at the pre-specified margin, or outcome performance is flat in marker-level fidelity — the latter would mean HEX's protein accuracy is not the mechanism by which MICA works, which would in turn invalidate the interpretability claims built on the predicted marker values. Failure modes: prospective accrual with same-section CODEX is slow and expensive, exactly the cost problem HEX exists to avoid; a single-institution prospective cohort would reproduce the generalization gap it is meant to close; marker-level fidelity and marker-level biological importance are correlated, so the stratified analysis may be confounded. Innovation status: unverified; prior-art search required.
